How it works

Back to the predictor

What goes in

Only the ten players and ten champions, one per role. No team names: any mix of players works. The model is a penalised logistic regression (ridge) with four ingredients:

  • Player ratings. Each player has an Elo-style rating built from every pro game they played since 2023, updated after each match day. The model uses blue's average rating minus red's.
  • Champion strength. Each champion has a learned weight from pro games; blue's picks add theirs and red's subtract.
  • Champion mastery. How much each player has played their champion in pro play across their whole career, with older games counting less (a 12-month half-life), grouped into five levels. The model uses the difference in average level.
  • Blue side. A fixed edge for the blue side, which wins slightly more often in pro play.

The ingredients add up to a score; the chance is 1 / (1 + e-score). "Show the math" on the prediction page lists this draft's numbers.

The curve over game time

The points at 20 to 40 minutes answer a narrower question: among games still going at that minute, how often does blue win? A second set of weights handles those minutes, so ratings and mastery can matter more or less as games get longer. The big pre-game number is the overall prediction.

Where the data comes from

Pro games from LCK, LPL, LEC and LCS (plus international events between their teams) from 2023 on, from gol.gg; player identities, roles and career histories back to 2011 from Leaguepedia. Lane win rates on the prediction page come from u.gg's Master+ solo queue statistics and are shown for context only: the model does not use them.

A high lane win rate can just mean one champion is overtuned that patch. So each lane also shows a counter edge: every champion gets an overall strength per role, fitted from all its matchups, and the edge is how much better (or worse) this particular matchup goes than those two strengths predict. An overtuned champion that beats everyone equally has no edge. Edges are combined across every stored patch, each weighted by how precise it is, so a patch with a handful of games barely counts. The edge shown is then pulled toward zero according to how noisy it is and how big real counters are in that lane (they are largest in top and mid, smallest in jungle and support), which predicted the next patch's matchups about 10% better than the raw edge.

What it does not know

  • Champion synergy, lane counters and draft chemistry: tested on pro games and found too noisy to help, so they are left out (for now).
  • Patch changes, recent form beyond the ratings, roster news, side selection strategy and in-game events.
  • Players who have not played pro games recently: their rating is as of their last match.
